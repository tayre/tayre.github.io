# Historical documentary production

Use this framework to produce an evidence-led film with restrained, cinematic visuals and a maintainable edit. Follow the user's current brief; defaults here are starting points, not additional authorization or fixed requirements.

Read the project's brief, decision log, recovery note and latest checkpoint before continuing an existing production. Inspect live jobs and saved outputs before restarting anything. For a new project, use [the starter prompt](STARTER-PROMPT.md) and [initializer](scripts/new_project.py).

## Route the work

- Research, script, archive selection, maps, narration or music: read [the production playbook](references/production-playbook.md).
- Environment, Blender setup, rendering, caching or recovery: read [technical setup](references/technical-setup.md).
- Critical review, revisions, export or delivery: read [review and delivery](references/review-delivery.md).
- Read only the templates relevant to the current stage; fill the working copies in the project.

## Production invariants

Keep historical assertions traceable to exact source locations. Distinguish contemporary testimony, later recollection, editorial interpretation and reconstruction. Correct OCR against the scan before quoting. Contextual archive footage must not imply that it depicts the named event or unit without evidence.

Make one representative map, archive/document treatment and voice sample reviewable before scaling production. Benchmark the intended render settings. Once a direction is accepted, continue authorized work without requesting the same approval again.

Preserve original sources, approved audio and completed scene masters. Treat labels, unit markers, arrows, titles and credits as editable layers where practical. A text or timing revision should normally recompose a short section, not rebuild the terrain or rerender the film.

Save a checkpoint before substantial edits, before and after export stages, and at handoff. Explain what the checkpoint covers and what depends on separately backed-up media. Do not treat the existence of a partial file as proof of completion.

Respect the project's money, disk, time and account-usage limits. Use live usage information when available; do not infer a remaining balance from the context window. Stop with a recovery note if a required limit cannot be verified or its agreed buffer is reached. Do not introduce purchases or recurring subscriptions beyond the user's authorization.

Report measured progress: completed clips and frames, active job, uncertainty in the estimate, current bottleneck, checkpoint and next step. Distinguish rendering, compositing, assembly and verification. Do not call a viewing copy broadcast-ready solely because it is high resolution or has passed a decode test.
