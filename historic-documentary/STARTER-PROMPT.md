# Historical documentary starter prompt

Copy the prompt below into a new production session. Replace the bracketed fields; leave optional decisions open if you want the assistant to propose them. The defaults are adjustable.

---

Create a historical documentary about **[subject, place and period]** for **[audience]**. The central question is **[what the viewer should understand]**. Aim for **[duration]**, with a firm maximum of **[duration]**. Read the `historic-documentary` framework and save the brief, research, decisions and project files in **[project directory]**.

Use primary sources, well-matched archival footage and photographs, geographically accurate Blender maps, natural narration and an understated musical score. The film should feel carefully written and edited: specific, human and visually coherent. Avoid generic dramatic claims, repetitive narration, excessive text and decorative effects without an explanatory purpose.

Creative direction:

- A clear opening question or situation, sufficient surrounding context, intelligible cause and consequence, and a conclusion that resolves the story.
- Slow, purposeful 3D map camera movement with a restrained isometric or oblique view. Terrain elevations must be credible. Selective focus may add depth, but labels and important actions must remain readable.
- Accurate symbols, dates, names and organizational relationships where relevant. Explain a change of scale from individual to unit, formation or region before replacing the visual focus.
- Sparse on-screen text, professional typography, gentle map-state transitions and enough time to read documents. Show authentic source material; use subtle crops and movement to guide attention.
- Archival shots selected for what they explain, with accurate contextual captions when the image differs from the narrated event. Prefer variety with a purpose and avoid accidental repeated shots.
- **[Voice preference; optional starting point: warm, low British voice with natural conversational pacing]**. Research place-name and personal-name pronunciation. Audition a short passage before recording the complete script. Do not slow speech mechanically to make it sound solemn.
- Music should support narration, allow quiet moments and resolve naturally at the end. Preserve separate narration, music and ambience stems.

Production constraints:

- Spending limit: **[amount and currency]**. Paid services and recurring commitments: **[allowed scope]**. Do not reuse a previous production's payment authorization.
- Delivery: **[private review, web release, festival or named broadcaster/specification]**. Verify current delivery and rights requirements for that destination.
- Computer and storage: **[hardware, working drive and protected free-space reserve]**.
- Account usage: leave **[percentage]** remaining, with an additional **[buffer]** before stopping. Report unavailable limits as unknown.
- Use existing suitable tools first; install only what the task needs within the authorized scope.
- Before expensive work, show a representative map motion sample, an archival/document treatment and a narration audition with a render-time estimate. After the direction is accepted, keep progressing within that scope.
- Save frequent checkpoints. Use restartable clips and retain approved versions. During editing, make targeted revisions and reuse finished 3D renders. Explain the scope and cost before any new long render.

Start by inspecting the workspace and available sources. Establish a source-backed narrative outline, a short script extract, a visual treatment, a rights register and a production plan. Ask only for information that materially blocks a decision; proceed with useful independent work meanwhile. Keep progress reports concrete and concise.

At completion, provide a full viewing copy, editable project/timeline, source and asset registers, captions, audio stems, recovery instructions and a clear statement of completed checks and any release limitations. Save general improvements back to the framework only when requested or already authorized.
