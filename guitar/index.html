02 / HOW THIS CHORD WORKS

A scale is a set of notes in order. Number them from 1 to 7, starting on the note that names the scale.

The notes in the chord

The numbers refer to the scale above. The root is note 1. A ♭ means lower that scale note by one fret; ♯ means raise a note by one fret.

Count along one string

One fret is one semitone: the smallest step between notes on a guitar. After 12 frets, the note name repeats at a higher pitch. That’s an octave.

03 / TRY A SCALE

A minor pentatonic

Standard tuning · frets 5–10

A chord plays several notes together. A scale gives you notes to play one at a time, for a melody or a solo. This one has just five: A, C, D, E and G. Try it over an A minor chord.

1 · rootA♭3C4D5E♭7G

“Pentatonic” means five notes. Start with A natural minor (A, B, C, D, E, F, G) and leave out B and F. The numbers above describe each note’s place relative to the A major scale, just like the chord formulas.

    A phrase to try

    Make a phrase, then take it somewhere else

    Use three or four notes to make a short phrase. Repeat it, change the last note, then leave a pause. Try ending on A to hear a clear stopping point. Once that feels comfortable, cross into the next position in the middle of a phrase. The boxes help you find notes; you can move between them whenever you like.

    For another step, try ending on a note from the chord being played. Over Am, try A, C or E. Over Dm, try D or A. Listen to how the same scale feels different when the chord changes.

    You can also move the whole pattern to a new key. Move every note two frets lower and the root on the low E string goes from A at fret 5 to G at fret 3: now you’re playing G minor pentatonic. Connecting the boxes above keeps you in A minor; shifting the entire pattern changes the key.

    01

    What does “root” mean?

    rootCthirdEfifthG

    The root names the chord. C major and C minor both start with C as their root. You can repeat these notes at different pitches and still have the same chord.

    02

    What changes in a minor chord?

    ↓

    E moves down one fret to E♭. C and G stay the same. That change in the third is what makes the chord minor. Play both to hear the difference.

    03

    Why learn more than one shape?

    fret 5A+12 fretsfret 17A

    Notes repeat along the neck. On the low E string, fret 5 is A and fret 17 is a higher A. New chord and scale shapes let you reach those other pitches without a big jump.
